# Wall-Tile Openings Conventions — Research for a Wall-Tile Openings Takeoff Feature

Date: 2026-09-29
Purpose: Ground how a wall-tile takeoff treats OPENINGS (doors, windows, niches, penetrations) and the trim, base and waterproofing items they generate, in real estimating and tile-setting practice.

## Q1 — Estimating: are openings deducted?

- **Yes, large openings are area-deducted.** Tile is area-priced, and trade practice nets out doors and windows. Small interruptions are **not** deducted; they are cut around, and the cutting cost comes back through waste. [AGG — tileprocalculator, badeloft]
- **Threshold:** the only numeric rule found is **"deduct openings larger than ~10 SF; leave outlets, small windows, valves and small niches in"** (the cuts waste about as much as the hole saves). One calculator uses **~2 SF** instead. Both come only from aggregator/calculator sites. **No [STD]/[MFR]/[EDU] source gives a threshold.** Treat it as a user-editable default, not a standard. [AGG — tileprocalculator, badeloft]
- **Area, not a cut count.** Openings reduce field SF. Their edges generate separate **LF** trim lines (returns, caps), and corners and connectors are listed as their own count lines. One practitioner blog puts it as "take off trim by opening and by edge… convert to linear feet… then add corners and connectors as their own line." [TRADE — Super Arbor]

## Q2 — Layout around openings

- **Center on the opening when it is the focal point.** ANSI A108.02 §4.3 says "center and balance areas of tile, if possible… usually, no cuts smaller than half size" (quoted via CTEF) [EDU]. The VA spec says "no tile less than one-half full size… Make all cuts on the outer edge of the field" [STD]. Trade guidance applies this to windows: center the window in the layout so that the cuts around it are equal and not slivers (HowToSpecialist asks for more than 2 in). [TRADE — HowToSpecialist, via search summary; site 403]
- **Conflict rule:** standards don't say whether wall-centering or opening-centering wins. That is a judgment call, the same gap as the corner-precedence gap in the 08-29 doc.
- **Cuts at opening corners:** tiles at the corners of an opening are **notched (L-cut)**. For complex openings, pros **template** the cuts: they tape trim profiles in place, build a template around each opening and batch-cut. [EDU — TileLetter "Templating and cutting"] No standard names the "L-cut". It is trade vocabulary.

## Q3 — Window/opening returns, sills, edge trim

- **Returns (jambs, head) are tiled faces.** Their area is **perimeter × return depth**. No source gives a standard depth, because it is the wall/frame depth taken from the drawings. The VA spec says to "form intersections and returns accurately." [STD — VA 09 30 13]
- **Edges:** "External corners including edges: Use bullnose shapes"; "Square internal and external corner joints are not acceptable". The VA spec also says to "use stop at bottom of openings having bullnose return to wall." [STD — VA] The metal alternative is **JOLLY / RONDEC / QUADEC**, "for tiled edges and outside corners", sold by length (**LF**). [MFR — Schluter]
- **Sill:** the spec treats a stone **window stool** as its own item. Section 09 30 13 covers "marble thresholds and window stools", a separate product line sized per the drawings. Thresholds are "one piece full width of door opening", so both are counted **EA** by size. [STD — VA] In showers, every sill must **slope to the drain**: "All horizontal surfaces, e.g. shower seats, sills, curbs… must slope towards drain" (TCNA, quoted), at **1/4–1/2 in per ft**. [EDU — NTCA/TileLetter, CTEF; MFR — Schluter handbook]
- **Units:** returns = SF (or LF × depth), edge bullnose/profile = LF, stool/threshold = EA, pre-formed corner/connector pieces = EA. [TRADE — Super Arbor; MFR — Schluter]

## Q4 — Recessed shower niches

- **Prefab:** Schluter KERDI-BOARD-SN comes in **12×6, 12×12, 12×20 and 12×28 in** sizes, is fastened to studs, and has a 2 in bonding flange sealed with an included KERDI-BAND frame. It is sold and counted **EA**. [MFR — Schluter; retailer pages]
- **Faces tiled:** the back plus 4 returns (sill, head, two jambs). The membrane covers the whole interior and laps onto the wall. [TRADE — Rubi, Contractors Direct] Schluter says openings in enclosed showers get membrane on "door jambs and the door header" too. [MFR]
- **Sill slope:** it must slope. The TCNA/NTCA figure for horizontal surfaces is 1/4–1/2 in/ft [EDU]. Trade blogs quote a smaller 1/16–1/8 in pitch [TRADE], which is a conflict to note. CTEF explicitly lists "the shelves of a niche… and a windowsill." [EDU — CTEF]
- **Edge trim:** bullnose, pencil/rail liner mitered 45° as a frame, or a metal profile. All are **LF** around the niche perimeter. [TRADE — Contractors Direct]

## Q5 — Penetrations (pipes, valves, outlets)

- **Not deducted; cut around.** The VA spec says to "fit tile closely around electrical outlets, piping, fixtures and fittings, so that plates, escutcheons, collars and flanges will overlap cut edge of tile," then seal the joints watertight. [STD — VA]
- **Waterproofing:** Schluter says "All penetrations must be treated with KERDI-SEAL-PS/-MV seals, KERDI-FIX or suitable sealant." PS is the pipe seal (showerhead, spout) and MV is the mixing-valve seal. Both are counted **EA per penetration**. [MFR — Schluter handbook]

## Q6 — Base, cove, wainscot cap

- The VA spec defines the corner shapes: "Base to floor internal corners: special shapes providing integral cove…"; "external corners: … bullnose vertical edge with integral cove"; and "wall top edge internal/external corners" need special shapes. [STD — VA] Submittals list "bullnose cap and base pieces at internal and external corners." [STD — VA]
- **Units:** base/cove and cap/pencil/chair rail = **LF**; inside/outside corner shapes = **EA**. Daltile sells cove base with left/right "Cove BC"/corner pieces. [MFR — Daltile via retailer listing] "At top of tile wainscot that finish flush with wall surfaces above use flat cap." [STD — VA]

## Q7 — Wet-area membrane

- **Height:** Schluter says "KERDI … shall be installed up to the height of the showerhead at minimum. In enclosed shower areas, install … on all surfaces, including the ceiling, door jambs and the door header." [MFR] TCNA B415 requires a membrane for walls (quoted via CTEF) [EDU]. B422 (bonding-flange drain) runs the membrane "to the shower head" (quoted via TileLetter) [EDU]. **IRC R307.2** sets the code minimum for the **nonabsorbent finish** at "not less than 6 feet above the floor" [STD]. That covers the finish, not the membrane. An AGG claim that B421 requires "72 in above the drain" **could not be verified** against a primary source.
- **SF:** membrane SF = shower walls up to max(showerhead height, tile height) + niche interior + returns + any ceiling. There is no standard deduction for penetrations.
- **Accessories:** seams need a 2 in overlap or KERDI-BAND (**LF**). Use "KERDI-KERECK at all inside and outside corners" (**EA**) and pipe/valve seals (**EA**). [MFR — Schluter handbook]

## Takeoff model implications

| Item | Unit | Deduct from field? |
|---|---|---|
| Door/window opening | SF deduction | Yes above a threshold; ~10 SF is an [AGG] default, user-editable |
| Outlet/pipe/valve | none (cut around) | No |
| Return (jamb/head/sill tile) | SF (perimeter × depth) | Adds area |
| Opening/niche edge trim | LF | — |
| Stool/threshold (stone) | EA by size | Replaces tile |
| Prefab niche | EA | Replaces field area |
| Base/cove, cap/liner | LF + corners EA | — |
| Membrane | SF + KERDI-BAND LF + corners EA + seals EA | — |
